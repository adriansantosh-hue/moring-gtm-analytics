# Moring GTM Strategy — Workflow-Led Demand

> **STATUS: ACTIVE STRATEGY (supersedes workshop-first CTA and AICP-led opening in earlier docs).**
> Provided by Moring leadership 2026-08-24. Where this doc conflicts with older playbooks, THIS wins.

Workflow-led demand. Governed production. Repeatable scale.

**STRATEGIC DECISION:** Lead most marketing, outbound and inbound conversations with a named business workflow, accountable owner and measurable outcome. Use AICP to prove that the agent can operate safely in production. Use AI-DLC to show how the first success becomes a repeatable enterprise capability.

This document defines what changes in the moring go-to-market motion, why the change is necessary, how the strategy applies across industries, what we pitch to each ICP, and the cold outreach frameworks for email and LinkedIn.

EVIDENCE BASE: Moring discovery conversations, vertical workflow research, installed GTM operating skills, and Google Cloud AI Agent Trends 2026. External figures are directional market evidence, not moring customer ROI.

---

## 1. The strategic decision

The strategy changes the order of the story, not the underlying product. Customers first need to recognize a workflow they own and a result they value. Once the workflow becomes real, AICP explains how the agent is controlled in production and AI-DLC explains how additional agents are built and improved consistently.

| Decision area | Previous emphasis | New emphasis | What remains true |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening message | AI Control Plane, AI-DLC and broad agentic capabilities | A named workflow, its owner, the current bottleneck and the desired outcome | AICP remains the production control layer |
| Primary proof | Architecture, platform capabilities and technical breadth | A workflow demo with source evidence, human decisions and a clear success measure | Technical proof follows business relevance |
| First CTA | Platform demo, generic workshop or POC discussion | **A short workflow diagnostic or comparison of the current process** | Workshop and paid validation remain later-stage offers |
| Commercial path | Sell the platform before the operating problem is qualified | Land one valuable workflow, prove control and expand to a governed portfolio | The long-term opportunity is still enterprise-wide AICP adoption |

### Why this change

| Evidence or observation | What it tells us | GTM implication |
|---|---|---|
| 52% of executives in organizations already using generative AI report agents in production | The conversation is shifting from experimentation to operating workflows | Lead with production-relevant workflows rather than general AI awareness |
| 88% of agentic AI early adopters report positive ROI on at least one generative AI use case | Buyers need a measurable use case, not a broad transformation promise | Define the workflow baseline and success measure before proposing a paid validation |
| Google defines agentic systems as human-guided, multi-step workflows | Enterprise value depends on orchestration across systems with accountable human decisions | Show the end-to-end workflow and the decision points, not only the agent interface |
| Major cloud platforms increasingly provide models, frameworks and integration protocols | Generic agent building and orchestration will become less differentiated | Differentiate through vertical workflows, governance, agent identity and production evidence |
| Moring discovery conversations consistently move toward specific business-unit workflows | Operating leaders recognize KYC, claims, lending, reconciliation, service and engineering problems faster than platform categories | Start with the buyer's process vocabulary |
| Governance becomes urgent when an agent accesses sensitive data or takes action | AICP is most persuasive after the workflow and risk are concrete | Introduce permissions, approvals, policies and auditability at the moment they matter |

Evidence note: the 52% and 88% figures are reported by Google Cloud from selected survey populations. They should not be treated as universal enterprise adoption or moring customer ROI.

### Operating principles

1. One account, one workflow hypothesis. Do not send a portfolio of generic capabilities in the first touch.
2. Name the business owner, human decision and measurable operating result before describing the platform.
3. Use signals to prioritize timing, not to substitute for ICP fit or confirmed business need.
4. Separate proposed, modeled, measured and realized outcomes in every customer-facing asset.
5. AICP-led selling remains appropriate when a trusted champion already owns an enterprise AI governance mandate or when the prospect already has multiple agents and pilots.

## 2. The new moring market story

**CORE POSITIONING:** moring builds governed **Business Workflow Agents** for high-value enterprise operations. AICP provides the identity, permissions, policies, human approvals, evaluations and auditability required to operate those agents safely. AI-DLC provides a repeatable path to design, test, release and improve them.

### What the customer buys and why

| Layer | Customer question | moring answer | Commercial role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business Workflow Agents | Which business process should AI improve now? | A governed agent for a named workflow with a responsible owner and defined outcome | Creates relevance and opens the account |
| AICP | How can this agent access data and act without creating uncontrolled risk? | Identity, access, policy, approval, evaluation, monitoring and audit controls | Creates production confidence and platform expansion |
| AI-DLC | How do we build and improve the next agents consistently? | A repeatable lifecycle for requirements, evaluation, release and change control | Creates repeatability and lowers the cost of scaling |
| FDE, workshop and paid validation | How do we move from the current process to a working production candidate? | Embedded discovery, workflow design, implementation and evidence collection | Converts interest into delivery and measurable proof |

### How the pitch should unfold

| Step | Prospect conversation | What moring shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Problem | Where is work delayed, inconsistent, expensive or difficult to audit? | The current workflow and its operating bottleneck |
| 2. Agent workflow | What evidence can an agent gather, compare or prepare? | A simple user flow showing agent work and human responsibility |
| 3. Outcome | What would improve if the workflow worked better? | Cycle time, analyst effort, backlog, quality, risk or customer response measures |
| 4. Production control | What must the agent never do without permission? | AICP identity, allowed actions, human approval, policy and audit trail |
| 5. Repeatability | How can the organization build the next workflow without starting again? | AI-DLC, reusable workflow blueprints and shared controls |
| 6. Expansion | Where else can the same governed foundation create value? | A portfolio of adjacent workflows under the same control model |

### Offer ladder

| Stage | Offer | Output | Commercial rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Workflow insight, benchmark, blog or short diagnostic | A useful view of one business problem | No generic platform brochure as the lead asset |
| Discovery | Workflow qualification call | Owner, current process, baseline, constraints and desired outcome | Do not prescribe a POC before this information exists |
| Design | Workflow-to-production workshop | Prioritized workflow, user flow, control requirements and validation plan | Workshop follows demonstrated relevance |
| Proof | Targeted demo or controlled prototype | A realistic workflow story with evidence, approvals and expected measures | Use synthetic data unless customer data is approved |
| Validation | Paid production validation | Measured workflow performance and go-live decision | Only after scope, owner, baseline and success criteria are agreed |
| Scale | AICP and AI-DLC expansion | Shared control plane and repeatable delivery model across workflows | Expand from proven workflow value |

## 3. Vertical and industry strategy

The vertical strategy should not become a catalogue of agents. Each campaign should choose one lead workflow that is important to the buyer, observable through a credible signal, and suitable for a governed human-in-the-loop design. Adjacent workflows support expansion only after the first problem resonates.

### How to choose a lead workflow

| Selection test | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| Business importance | The workflow affects revenue, cost, risk, customer experience or a non-negotiable deadline |
| Operational friction | People currently gather evidence across systems, reconcile conflicts or manage large exception queues |
| Human accountability | A named role still owns the final approval or judgment |
| Production suitability | The agent can assist meaningfully without requiring uncontrolled autonomy |
| Repeatability | The same pattern can be reused across accounts, business units or adjacent workflows |

### Regulated and mission-critical markets

| Market | Lead workflow | Expansion workflows | Buyer and opening outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Banking and financial services | Commercial onboarding and KYC evidence | AML investigation, lending exceptions, investment-data reconciliation, payment disputes and fraud | KYC, compliance or operations leader. Reduce evidence chasing and case cycle time while preserving named approval and a complete record. |
| Insurance | Claims triage and evidence assembly | Underwriting evidence, policy-servicing exceptions, SIU investigations and prior authorization | Claims or underwriting leader. Reduce manual review and route complex cases with the evidence and rationale intact. |
| Healthcare | Prior authorization evidence and routing | Claims denials, revenue-cycle exceptions, referrals, scheduling and member service | Clinical operations or revenue-cycle leader. Reduce administrative delay while keeping clinical and regulated decisions with the responsible human. |
| Aerospace and aviation | Maintenance troubleshooting evidence | AOG parts, service-bulletin impact, nonconformance, CAPA and compliance reporting | Maintenance or engineering leader. Reduce time to evidence while preserving airworthiness authority and approved procedures. |
| Energy and utilities | Outage restoration coordination | Asset inspection, grid constraints, field safety, service dispatch and billing exceptions | Operations or field-service leader. Accelerate response across systems while keeping safety and restoration decisions controlled. |

### Operational and technology-led markets

| Market | Lead workflow | Expansion workflows | Buyer and opening outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manufacturing | Warranty and field-service resolution | Predictive maintenance, quality exceptions, production scheduling and parts shortages | Service or operations leader. Assemble evidence faster, recommend next action and preserve engineering approval. |
| Retail and ecommerce | Returns and refund exception handling | Refund fraud, inventory exceptions, pricing, order resolution and supplier invoices | Customer operations or ecommerce leader. Resolve exceptions faster without allowing uncontrolled credits or refunds. |
| Logistics and supply chain | Disruption and exception management | Demand exceptions, rerouting, warehouse issues, supplier risk and expediting | Supply-chain or logistics leader. Detect the issue, coordinate systems and recommend recovery before the customer escalates. |
| Telecom | Service assurance and incident coordination | Network correlation, root-cause analysis, field dispatch, revenue assurance and capacity | Network or service operations leader. Connect alerts, tickets, dispatch and customer communication in one governed flow. |
| SaaS and software engineering | Release evidence and quality gates | Requirements, architecture, code changes, testing, incident evidence and vulnerability remediation | Engineering or platform leader. Increase development throughput without bypassing security, review and release controls. |

**PRIORITIZATION RULE:** Start with BFSI and other regulated workflows where human approvals, evidence and auditability are intrinsic to the business process. Use manufacturing, retail, logistics, telecom and software engineering where cycle time and cross-system coordination create a clear operating case. Do not activate every vertical at once.

## 4. What we pitch to each ICP

The same workflow must be translated into the language of each member of the buying committee. The message changes by role, but the underlying workflow and outcome remain consistent.

| ICP | What they care about | What we pitch | First CTA and why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic outcome buyer: CEO, COO, business-unit leader, transformation executive | Growth, cost, operating leverage, customer outcomes and execution risk | A high-value workflow that can be improved without removing accountable human ownership. AICP appears as the reason the change can reach production safely. | A short workflow and value discussion. This buyer needs a business case before architecture. |
| Workflow owner: operations, claims, KYC, lending, service, finance or engineering leader | Backlog, cycle time, analyst effort, exception quality, SLA and control failures | An agent that gathers evidence, compares sources, prepares a recommendation and stops at the right human decision. | A current-state workflow diagnostic. This buyer can validate the problem, baseline and user flow. |
| Control and platform buyer: CIO, CTO, CISO, Chief Data or AI Officer, risk and governance | Identity, access, policy, data boundaries, evaluation, audit, interoperability and scale | AICP as the shared control layer for agents across workflows, models and systems. AI-DLC as the repeatable lifecycle for change. | A production-control review tied to a real workflow. This avoids an abstract platform discussion. |
| Partner or systems integrator | Differentiated offerings, faster delivery, reusable IP, client trust and revenue expansion | A joint workflow solution where the partner brings customer access and implementation context, while moring provides governed agent delivery, AICP and reusable blueprints. | A partnership-fit discussion around one customer workflow. This creates a tangible co-sell opportunity. |

### Enterprise and mid-market routes

| Dimension | Enterprise motion | Mid-market motion |
|---|---|---|
| Opening problem | Cross-system workflow, regulated decision, fragmented ownership or multiple pilots | A visible backlog, manual process, customer delay or capacity constraint |
| Buying group | Multi-thread business owner, control buyer, technical owner and executive sponsor | Workflow owner plus technical or executive approver |
| Proof needed | Governance, architecture fit, data boundaries, approvals, audit and enterprise rollout path | Fast operational value, integration practicality, implementation effort and payback |
| Best first offer | Workflow diagnostic followed by a focused workshop or controlled demo | Short discovery followed by a use-case demo and tightly scoped validation |
| AICP timing | Introduce early when multiple agents, regulated actions or a governance mandate already exist | Introduce after the first workflow value is clear unless the buyer is explicitly platform-led |

### When AICP should lead

- A trusted internal champion already owns enterprise AI governance or platform strategy.
- The prospect has several pilots or agents and lacks common identity, policy, evaluation or audit controls.
- The initial buyer is a CIO, CISO, Chief Data or AI Officer, enterprise architect, risk leader or governance owner.
- The immediate decision is about a shared production foundation rather than one business-unit workflow.

In all other cases, use the workflow to earn attention and use AICP to answer the production question.

## 5. Outbound strategy

**ACCOUNT QUALIFICATION FORMULA:** ICP fit + credible workflow pain + timely signal + reachable buying committee + clear exclusions. A signal without workflow fit is not a qualified account.

### Account and signal strategy

| Signal | What it may indicate | How to use it | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| Former customer or alumni user | Existing familiarity and a shorter trust-building path | Reference the known problem or prior operating context without overstating advocacy | Verify the relationship and prior value before outreach |
| New leader in the first 90 days | A mandate to improve a function and willingness to reconsider tools | Connect the leader's remit to one workflow they now own | Do not assume an active project |
| Workflow-specific hiring | Capacity pressure, transformation work or a capability gap | Use the role to form a workflow hypothesis and cost comparison | Clay/Other tools is the required source of record for live hiring signals |
| AI or modernization initiative | Budget, sponsorship or active technology change | Position the workflow as a production outcome, not another isolated pilot | Confirm the initiative and responsible function |
| Regulatory, service or operational event | A dated reason to review controls, cycle time or customer response | Use the event as context for the workflow problem | Use only public, company-specific and recent evidence |
| First-party website or content intent | Active interest in a topic or problem | Prioritize fast human review and route to the relevant workflow owner | Intent shows timing, not fit, budget or buyer authority |

### Account coverage

| Route | Typical roles | Message focus |
|---|---|---|
| Business outcome | COO, BU leader, transformation leader | Why this workflow matters to growth, cost, customer experience or risk |
| Workflow owner | Operations, claims, KYC, lending, service, finance or engineering leader | Where time is lost, what the agent prepares and what the human still decides |
| Technical and control | CIO, CTO, CISO, AI platform, data, architecture, risk and governance | Identity, permissions, policy, evaluation, auditability and integration |
| Executive sponsor | CEO, regional leader, portfolio or practice leader | Strategic value, organizational path and expansion potential |

### Campaign operating sequence

1. Select one vertical, one workflow and one primary ICP for the campaign.
2. Research the account and write a one-sentence workflow hypothesis supported by a dated signal.
3. Map three to five people across business, workflow, technical-control and executive routes.
4. Create route-specific messages that share the same workflow thesis.
5. Offer a useful comparison, diagnostic or short workflow asset before asking for a workshop.
6. Qualify the current process, owner, baseline, data and approval boundary in discovery.
7. Move to a demo, workshop or paid production validation only when the workflow is confirmed.

### Content and offer strategy

| Asset | Purpose | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Workflow insight or blog | Explain a specific operating problem, user flow and measurable outcome | Awareness, outbound follow-up and inbound capture |
| Two-page workflow brief | Show problem, agent-assisted process, human decision, controls and modeled value | After relevance is established or when the prospect asks for details |
| Workflow-to-production workshop | Prioritize one workflow and define the production path | After discovery with the owner and technical-control stakeholders |
| Targeted demo | Make the workflow and human approval visible with realistic evidence | When the prospect needs to understand how the solution works |
| AICP proof sheet | Explain identity, permissions, policy, evaluation and auditability | When production risk or cross-agent scale becomes a buying question |
| Case study or value model | Connect workflow change to measured or clearly modeled outcomes | Later-stage business case and paid validation decision |

## 6. Cold email framework

**MESSAGE FORMULA:** Verified signal or relevant observation → to named workflow problem → to specific agent-assisted change → to human control → to one low-friction question.

### Copy by ICP

| Recipient | Length and emphasis | Message direction |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic outcome buyer | Two to three short sentences. Lead with business impact and execution risk. | We see a specific workflow limiting operating capacity. moring can improve it while preserving accountable decisions. Is it relevant to compare the current process? |
| Workflow owner | Three to four short sentences. Use the exact process, evidence and handoff. | The agent gathers and compares the information, prepares the recommendation and stops for the owner's decision. Ask how the process works today. |
| Control or platform buyer | Two to three short sentences. Lead with production control across real agents. | Tie identity, permissions, policy and audit to a specific workflow. Avoid a generic governance pitch. |
| Partner | Three short sentences. Lead with a client workflow and joint commercial value. | Show what the partner can take to its customers, what moring contributes and why the combined offer is differentiated. |

### Four-email sequence

| Touch | Objective | Content | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email 1: relevance | Earn a response | Signal, workflow observation, specific consequence and an open question | No links or attachments. Do not lead with a product list. |
| Email 2: business case | Make the cost of the status quo clearer | Pain, KPI or operational outcome, plus one concise example | Send about three days after Email 1. |
| Email 3: route and proof | Reach the right owner or add credibility | A short workflow asset, case pattern or delegation question | Use a new subject line and send after a meaningful gap. |
| Email 4: clean close | Create a simple yes, no or later decision | Ask whether the workflow is not a priority now or belongs with someone else | Leave the door open and stop the sequence. |

### First-email template

```
Subject: [workflow] at [company]

Hi [First name],

[Verified signal or relevant observation]. Teams handling [workflow] often lose time to [specific evidence, exception or handoff problem].

moring is building a governed agent that [specific action], while [named human role] keeps the final decision. Would it be useful to compare how [company] handles this today?

Regards,
Nikhil
```

### Preflight rules

- Pass SPF, DKIM and DMARC checks before any send.
- Verify addresses and remove invalid or uncertain contacts before launch.
- Use no links or attachments in the first cold email.
- Ensure every variable has a safe fallback and preview rendered messages.
- Ramp volume by mailbox and set bounce and placement kill-switches before launch.
- Keep campaigns paused until list, copy, sender and approval checks are complete.

## 7. LinkedIn outreach framework

LinkedIn should feel like a professional conversation, not a compressed email sequence. The connection request opens the relationship. The first message earns relevance. The asset or meeting request follows only after the workflow is understood.

| Stage | Objective | Example | Timing rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Connection request | Establish relevance without pitching | Hi [First], I am comparing how [vertical] teams handle [workflow] as AI moves into production. Open to connecting? | Short, specific and no meeting request. |
| Post-acceptance message | Start a workflow conversation | Thanks, [First]. We are working on a governed agent for [workflow] that [specific action], while [role] keeps final approval. Is this a workflow [company] is actively reviewing? | Send after acceptance, not immediately as an automated wall of text. |
| Value follow-up | Offer a useful point of view | We put together a short view of how [workflow] can move from manual review to controlled agent assistance, including the approval and audit steps. Happy to share it if useful. | Share the asset only if relevant or requested. |
| Meeting request | Move an engaged conversation to discovery | If this is relevant, would a 20-minute workflow discussion next week be useful? | Ask only after a response or clear engagement. |

### LinkedIn message rules

- Do not pitch in the connection request.
- Use the prospect's workflow language rather than moring product terminology.
- Keep each message to one idea and one question.
- Do not paste a research packet or multiple proof points into a LinkedIn message.
- Use profile or account signals only when they are verified and naturally relevant.
- For enterprise accounts, build credibility across the buying committee before asking for a meeting.
- For mid-market accounts, move to a workflow demo faster when the owner confirms a real problem.

### Channel coordination

| Situation | Recommended action |
|---|---|
| Connected on LinkedIn, no conversation | Start with a workflow observation. Do not immediately duplicate the cold email. |
| Email opened or clicked, no reply | Do not claim to have tracked the behavior. Use LinkedIn only if there is an independent reason to connect. |
| LinkedIn reply shows interest | Pause automated touches and continue as a human conversation. |
| Wrong person identified | Use a concise delegation question and route the account rather than continuing the full sequence. |
| Prospect requests material | Send the smallest relevant workflow brief, then ask one question that advances qualification. |

## 8. Measurement and review

The strategy should be evaluated by progression from relevance to a qualified workflow, not by activity volume alone. Establish current baselines before setting hard performance targets.

| Stage | Primary measure | What it diagnoses |
|---|---|---|
| Research quality | Share of accounts with confirmed ICP fit, one workflow hypothesis, a credible signal and complete exclusions | Whether targeting is valid before copy performance is judged |
| Message relevance | Positive reply rate by vertical, workflow, ICP and signal | Whether the workflow and problem resonate |
| Meeting quality | Share of meetings with a confirmed owner, current process and business consequence | Whether responses convert into real opportunities |
| Workflow qualification | Share with a baseline, data sources, human decision and success measure | Whether a demo, workshop or validation can be scoped responsibly |
| Commercial progression | Discovery to workshop or demo, then to paid validation | Whether the offer ladder is working |
| Expansion | Validated workflow to AICP or additional workflow adoption | Whether workflow-led entry creates platform value |

### Experiment discipline

- Test one meaningful variable at a time: workflow, ICP route, signal or message angle.
- Review results by vertical and ICP. A blended response rate can hide a weak market-message fit.
- Record why prospects reply, object, delegate or remain silent. Use those patterns to update the workflow narrative.
- Do not convert proposed or modeled outcome ranges into realized ROI without a measured customer baseline.
- Pause or narrow campaigns when list quality, deliverability, message relevance or routing fails.
