# BFSI Vertical Outbound Playbook
### Banking, Financial Institutions and Insurance
### United States, UAE and Australia | Enterprise and Mid-Market

**Scope:** Vertical-specific account selection, workflow campaigns, ICP routes, email and LinkedIn cadence, and content placement. Use the master Moring GTM strategy for company-level positioning, qualification principles and measurement framework.

This playbook contains only the banking, financial institutions and insurance execution layer. It does not repeat the company-wide GTM rationale or general channel guidance already covered in the master strategy.

---

## 1. How the previous campaigns change

The previous structure asked prospects to understand Moring product categories before they had a reason to care. The new structure starts with a workflow they already own. The product layers appear only after the workflow problem is established.

| Previous campaign | What remains useful | New role in outbound |
|---|---|---|
| **AICP** | Strong for CIO, CISO, risk, architecture and warm champion-led conversations. | Supporting narrative inside every workflow campaign. Lead only when a known champion already recognizes the governance problem. |
| **AI-DLC** | Strong for CTO, CIO, VP Engineering and platform teams building AI-enabled software. | A specialist campaign route triggered by engineering hiring, AI release bottlenecks or software-delivery transformation. |
| **Agentic Solutions** | Closest to how operating leaders describe demand. | Becomes the primary motion, renamed and packaged as **Business Workflow Agents**. |

## 2. Account universe and selection data

### Priority account types

| Vertical | Priority account types | Important operating metric |
|---|---|---|
| **Banking & Financial Institutions** | Commercial and regional banks; retail banks; credit unions or mutuals; asset and wealth managers; custodians; payments and lending platforms. | Total assets, AUM, transaction volume, loan book, branches, customer base and regulated entities. |
| **Insurance** | Property and casualty or general insurance; life and annuity; health or private health; reinsurers; specialty carriers; MGAs, brokers and TPAs with meaningful operational scale. | Gross written premium, claims volume, policies in force, lines of business, distribution model and regulated entities. |

### Selection fields and thresholds

**Selection principle:** Company size is necessary but not sufficient. An account enters outbound only when scale, workflow fit, timing and buyer accessibility are all present.

### Recommended segment thresholds

| Market | Enterprise starting threshold | Mid-market starting threshold |
|---|---|---|
| **United States** | 2,000+ employees, or $1B+ revenue, or $25B+ banking assets/AUM, or $1B+ insurance premium. | 300 to 1,999 employees, or $100M to $999M revenue, or $2B to $24.9B assets/AUM, or $100M to $999M insurance premium. |
| **UAE** | 1,000+ employees, or $500M+ revenue, or $10B+ banking assets/AUM, or $500M+ insurance premium. | 200 to 999 employees, or $50M to $499M revenue, or $1B to $9.9B assets/AUM, or $50M to $499M insurance premium. |
| **Australia** | 1,500+ employees, or $750M+ revenue, or $15B+ banking assets/AUM, or $750M+ insurance premium. | 250 to 1,499 employees, or $75M to $749M revenue, or $1.5B to $14.9B assets/AUM, or $75M to $749M insurance premium. |

Use an **OR rule for scale**, then apply human review. Vertical metrics such as assets, AUM and premium should override headcount when they better represent operational complexity. Recalibrate thresholds after the first 100 reviewed accounts in each market.

### Mandatory company fields

| Field group | Fields to capture | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Identity and geography** | Company name, domain, headquarters, operating countries, regulated entities, parent company and ownership type. | Prevents duplicate accounts and supports local messaging. |
| **Industry and subsegment** | Bank, lender, payments, asset manager, custodian, insurer, reinsurer, broker, MGA, TPA and lines of business. | Determines which workflow is credible. |
| **Scale** | Employees, revenue, assets, AUM, gross written premium, branches, customers, policies, claims or transaction volume where available. | Separates enterprise and mid-market and estimates workflow scale. |
| **Workflow fit** | Current workflow, process owner, queue or exception type, likely systems, manual steps and measurable operating KPI. | Provides the actual reason to contact the account. |
| **Technology and AI maturity** | Cloud, data platforms, AI stack, automation tools, public AI initiatives, pilots and production deployments. | Determines whether AICP, AI-DLC or implementation detail should appear. |
| **Risk and regulatory exposure** | Sensitive data, customer decisions, approval requirements, audit obligations and material jurisdictions. | Defines the control narrative and risk buyer. |
| **Timing signals** | Leadership change, transformation programme, new product or region, regulatory deadline, technology change, hiring, vendor review and content engagement. | Creates a defensible why-now. |
| **Relationship and routing** | Account owner, partner path, prior meetings, active opportunity, customer history, mutual connection and suppression status. | Prevents duplicate outreach and supports warm routes. |
| **Data quality** | Source URL, source date, confidence, last verified date and unresolved conflicts. | Makes the dataset auditable and refreshable. |

## 3. Workflow priorities by market

The table below defines the first workflow hypotheses. Account research must confirm the workflow before outreach. Do not contact every company with the same generic agent story.

| Market | Banking & Financial Institutions | Insurance |
|---|---|---|
| **United States** | Commercial onboarding and KYC evidence; AML and financial-crime investigations; lending exceptions; investment-data reconciliation for asset, wealth and custody firms; payment disputes and fraud. | Claims evidence and exception resolution; underwriting evidence; policy-servicing exceptions; SIU investigations; health-insurance prior authorization where relevant. |
| **UAE** | Commercial onboarding and KYC; sanctions and AML review; trade-finance document evidence; credit exceptions; payment disputes and fraud. | Motor and health claims; underwriting evidence; policy servicing; claims-integrity and fraud investigations; regulatory evidence preparation. |
| **Australia** | Customer onboarding and KYC; financial-crime investigation; lending exceptions; hardship and complaints evidence; investment or superannuation operations reconciliation. | Claims evidence and exception resolution; underwriting; policy servicing; claims-integrity investigations; complaints and regulatory evidence. |

### Recommended first campaign in each market

| Market | Banking or FI starting wedge | Insurance starting wedge |
|---|---|---|
| **United States** | Commercial onboarding and KYC. Use investment-data reconciliation as the specialist branch for asset managers and custodians. | Claims evidence and exception resolution. |
| **UAE** | Commercial onboarding, KYC and sanctions evidence. | Motor or health claims evidence, selected by the carrier mix. |
| **Australia** | Financial-crime investigation or lending exceptions, selected by the account type. | Claims evidence and complaints resolution. |

## 4. Workflow campaigns

### Campaigns to build

| Campaign | Primary problem | Primary ICP | Supporting narrative |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Banking 1: Onboarding and KYC evidence** | Analysts chase evidence across sources, resolve ownership conflicts and wait for approval. | Head of Client Onboarding, KYC Operations or Customer Due Diligence. | AICP controls source access, sensitive data, approvals and the decision record. |
| **Banking 2: Financial-crime investigations** | Investigators assemble alerts, transactions, entities and policy evidence manually. | Head of AML Operations, Financial Crime or Investigations. | AICP constrains access and actions, records evidence and keeps final decisions with accountable teams. |
| **Banking 3: Operations exceptions** | Lending, payment or investment-data exceptions require repeated cross-system checks. | Head of Lending Operations, Payments Operations, Reconciliation or Investment Operations. | AICP provides bounded tools, human approval and traceable recommendations. |
| **Insurance 1: Claims evidence and exceptions** | Claims teams gather documents, identify missing evidence and route complex cases manually. | Chief Claims Officer, Head of Claims Operations or Claims Transformation. | AICP protects customer data and records evidence, recommendations and approvals. |
| **Insurance 2: Underwriting evidence** | Underwriters spend time collecting risk evidence and reconciling inconsistent sources. | Chief Underwriting Officer or Head of Underwriting Operations. | AICP controls approved sources, decision support and escalation. |
| **Insurance 3: Policy, complaints and fraud** | Servicing, complaints and SIU cases move across fragmented systems and teams. | Head of Policy Operations, Complaints, SIU or Claims Integrity. | AICP provides controlled access, routing, evidence and auditability. |

## 5. ICP routes and pitch

| ICP route | Typical banking and FI titles | Typical insurance titles | What they care about |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Workflow and operations** | COO; Head of Operations; Client Onboarding; KYC Operations; Lending, Payments, Reconciliation or Investment Operations. | COO; Chief Claims Officer; Claims Operations; Chief Underwriting Officer; Policy Operations; SIU or Complaints. | Queue size, turnaround time, manual effort, rework, service quality and accountable ownership. |
| **2. Risk, compliance and governance** | Chief Risk Officer; Chief Compliance Officer; MLRO; Head of Financial Crime; Model Risk; Responsible AI. | Chief Risk Officer; Chief Compliance Officer; Claims Integrity; Privacy; Responsible AI; Internal Audit. | Evidence, policy compliance, approval boundaries, traceability, sensitive data and audit readiness. |
| **3. Technology, data and AI** | CIO; CTO; CDAO; Head of AI; Enterprise Architecture; AI Platform; Data Platform. | CIO; CTO; CDAO; Head of AI; Enterprise Architecture; Data and Integration. | Integration, reusable controls, production ownership, platform fit, security and scaling across workflows. |
| **4. Engineering and software delivery** | VP Engineering; Head of Platform Engineering; Application Development; DevSecOps; AI Engineering. | VP Engineering; Head of Digital Engineering; Platform Engineering; Application Development; DevSecOps. | Build, test, release and change AI-enabled software with fewer delivery bottlenecks. |

### Primary executive ICP profiles

| Profile | What they own and when to target | Correct Moring pitch |
|---|---|---|
| **COO / Head of Operations** | Owns service levels, cost, queues and operating performance. Target when onboarding, claims, lending or reconciliation work is slow, manual or inconsistent. | Lead with one Business Workflow Agent, its baseline and expected outcome. Show human approval and AICP controls as part of production. |
| **CRO / CCO / Compliance leader** | Owns policy, operational risk, model risk, sensitive data and audit evidence. Target when AI use cases cannot clear review or their actions cannot be explained. | Lead with the governed workflow. Show controlled access, decision boundaries, evidence, monitoring and auditability through AICP. |
| **CIO** | Owns enterprise technology strategy, integration, security, vendor fit and reuse. Target when AI pilots are fragmented or duplicate infrastructure. | Start with one high-value workflow, then show AICP as the shared production control layer that fits existing systems and cloud requirements. |
| **CTO / Enterprise Architecture** | Owns architecture standards, platform design, scalability and reliability. Target when agent designs are hard to standardize or move safely into production. | Show the workflow architecture, bounded tools and human decision points. Position AICP as reusable technical controls. Introduce AI-DLC only for software-delivery needs. |
| **CDAO / Head of AI** | Owns AI portfolio value, data access, evaluation, reuse and model governance. Target when pilots lack prioritization, consistent evaluation or accountable workflow owners. | Prioritize named workflows with owners and measurable outcomes. Use AICP to govern deployment and evidence across the portfolio. |
| **VP Engineering / Platform leader** | Owns engineering throughput, developer experience, release quality and platform reliability. Target when AI-assisted delivery needs testing, release gates and evidence. | Lead with a specific software-delivery workflow. Position AI-DLC as the delivery method and AICP as the access, policy, evidence and production-control layer. |

### Enterprise and mid-market differences

| Dimension | Enterprise | Mid-market |
|---|---|---|
| **Buying committee** | Multi-thread operations, risk, technology and often engineering. | Usually one operating sponsor plus one technology or risk approver. |
| **Opening angle** | Workflow scale, governance across teams and a reusable production pattern. | Backlog, cost, turnaround time and getting one workflow live without a large platform programme. |
| **Proof requirement** | Architecture confidence, deployment model, controls, customer proof and stakeholder alignment. | Clear use case, time to value, ownership, implementation effort and commercial clarity. |
| **Primary CTA** | Tailored workflow discussion or workshop with business, technology and risk. | Short discovery and targeted demo, then a scoped paid validation. |
| **Personalization depth** | Account research plus role-specific message for 3 to 4 contacts. | Account research plus role-specific message for 2 contacts. |

## 6. Outbound cadence

### Enterprise sequence: 5 emails and 4 LinkedIn touches

| Timing | Channel | What the touch should communicate | Asset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Email 1 | Company-specific workflow observation, likely operational friction and one question about ownership or priority. | No link. No attachment. |
| Day 3 | LinkedIn 1 | Connection request based on the workflow or their remit. Do not pitch the full product. | No link. |
| Day 5 | Email 2 | Explain one consequence of the workflow problem and share a useful point of view. | Workflow-specific blog link. |
| Day 8 | LinkedIn 2 | After acceptance, ask a short question about how the workflow is handled today. | No link. |
| Day 11 | Email 3 | Show how the workflow agent would gather evidence, prepare a recommendation and keep the accountable person in control. | Use-case brief for operations. AICP whitepaper for risk or technology. AI-DLC article for engineering. |
| Day 15 | LinkedIn 3 | Share one relevant observation or compact example. Ask whether the issue sits with them or another owner. | Send a link only after engagement or permission. |
| Day 18 | Email 4 | Invite the buyer and relevant stakeholders to a workflow workshop or targeted demo. Explain what the session will produce. | Workshop landing-page link. |
| Day 24 | LinkedIn 4 | Short meeting request or polite redirect to the correct owner. | No link unless requested. |
| Day 28 | Email 5 | Close the loop, ask whether the workflow is not a priority or belongs with someone else. | No link. |

### Mid-market sequence: 4 emails and 3 LinkedIn touches

| Timing | Channel | What the touch should communicate | Asset |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 1 | Email 1 | Specific workflow problem, operating impact and a simple relevance question. | No link. No attachment. |
| Day 3 | LinkedIn 1 | Connection request tied to their role and workflow. | No link. |
| Day 5 | Email 2 | Explain the cost or turnaround-time consequence and how a bounded agent reduces manual work. | Workflow-specific blog link. |
| Day 8 | LinkedIn 2 | Ask how the process is handled and whether improvement is already planned. | No link. |
| Day 11 | Email 3 | Show the simple user flow, implementation boundary and the outcome that would be measured. Invite a targeted demo or workshop. | Use-case brief plus workshop link. Whitepaper only for technology or risk. |
| Day 16 | LinkedIn 3 | Short meeting request or referral question. | No link unless requested. |
| Day 20 | Email 4 | Close the loop and ask whether the timing or owner is wrong. | No link. |

## 7. Message content by ICP

The cadence stays consistent. The substance changes by buyer route. Every message must name a workflow that fits the account.

### ICP 1: Workflow and operations

| Touch | Message focus | Enterprise adjustment | Mid-market adjustment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email 1 | A queue, exception or evidence-gathering problem owned by their team. | Emphasize scale across teams, products or jurisdictions. | Emphasize backlog, manual effort and a fast first workflow. |
| Email 2 | Why manual evidence gathering or exception handling creates delay and rework. | Share a vertical workflow blog with operating and control implications. | Share a practical blog focused on time and effort. |
| Email 3 | Simple user flow: agent gathers approved evidence, prepares work and stops for human decision. | Add a short use-case brief and explain cross-functional control. | Show the smallest deployable workflow and success metric. |
| Email 4 | Invite operations, technology and risk to define the workflow and baseline. | Workshop link and expected outputs. | Targeted demo or compact workshop link. |
| Email 5 | Priority or ownership check. | Ask for the correct workflow owner. | Omit this touch and close in email 4. |
| LinkedIn | Connect on workflow relevance, ask how it is handled, share one observation, request a conversation. | Four touches, no link before engagement. | Three touches, short and direct. |

### ICP 2: Risk, compliance and governance

| Touch | Message focus | Enterprise adjustment | Mid-market adjustment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email 1 | The difficulty of proving what an AI-assisted workflow accessed, recommended and escalated. | Emphasize policy consistency, multiple stakeholders and audit evidence. | Emphasize approval boundaries and avoiding manual review overhead. |
| Email 2 | Why governance added after implementation slows production. | Share a workflow-governance blog. | Share a short practical governance article. |
| Email 3 | AICP controls: approved data and tools, human decisions, evidence, monitoring and audit trail. | AICP whitepaper link. | Short AICP overview or relevant whitepaper section. |
| Email 4 | Invite risk, business and technology to map one governed workflow. | Workshop link. | Targeted governance discussion or workshop link. |
| Email 5 | Ask whether the topic belongs to Responsible AI, compliance, model risk or technology risk. | Use as a routing question. | Omit and close in email 4. |
| LinkedIn | Connect around responsible deployment, ask how controls are applied, then request a short discussion. | Four touches. Link only after engagement. | Three touches. Keep the language practical. |

### ICP 3: Technology, data and AI

| Touch | Message focus | Enterprise adjustment | Mid-market adjustment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email 1 | A named workflow that needs integration, controls and accountable production ownership. | Emphasize reuse across business units without forcing one agent framework. | Emphasize getting one workflow live without building a large platform first. |
| Email 2 | The gap between a successful prototype and a supportable production workflow. | Share a blog about taking one workflow into governed production. | Share a practical production-readiness article. |
| Email 3 | How AICP provides common access, approvals, evidence, monitoring and auditability. | AICP whitepaper link. | Short architecture overview or selected whitepaper section. |
| Email 4 | Invite a workflow and architecture workshop. | Include business, risk and architecture. Workshop link. | Targeted technical demo and scoping call. |
| Email 5 | Ask whether AI platform, enterprise architecture or the workflow owner should evaluate it. | Use for routing. | Omit and close in email 4. |
| LinkedIn | Connect on production ownership, ask about current pattern, share one relevant example, request a call. | Four touches. | Three touches. |

### ICP 4: Engineering and software delivery

| Touch | Message focus | Enterprise adjustment | Mid-market adjustment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Email 1 | A specific AI-enabled software delivery bottleneck: requirements, testing, release evidence or production change. | Emphasize engineering scale, standards and release gates. | Emphasize developer time, release speed and avoiding a separate AI delivery process. |
| Email 2 | Why normal SDLC practices need additional AI evaluation, evidence and controls. | Share an AI engineering or AI-DLC blog. | Share a concise implementation article. |
| Email 3 | AI-DLC as the method for building, testing, releasing and changing AI-assisted software. | AI-DLC technical brief or whitepaper. | Compact AI-DLC overview. |
| Email 4 | Invite engineering, architecture and risk to map one software-delivery workflow. | Workshop link. | Targeted engineering discussion. |
| Email 5 | Ask whether platform engineering, application development or AI engineering owns the issue. | Use for routing. | Omit and close in email 4. |
| LinkedIn | Connect on engineering workflow, ask about the release process, then request a short discussion. | Four touches. | Three touches. |

## 8. Asset placement

| Asset | Where it belongs | What it must contain | Do not use it when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Workflow blog** | Email 2. LinkedIn only after engagement. | The buyer problem, why it persists, a simple workflow pattern and practical implications. | The article is generic, platform-first or unrelated to the named workflow. |
| **AICP whitepaper** | Email 3 for risk, technology and governance buyers. | Access, approvals, evidence, monitoring, auditability and customer-cloud deployment. | The buyer is an operations leader who has not yet accepted the workflow problem. |
| **AI-DLC article or brief** | Email 2 or 3 for engineering buyers only. | Requirements, evaluation, testing, release gates, evidence and change management for AI-assisted software. | The account has no engineering-led AI delivery problem. |
| **Use-case brief or case study** | Email 3 for operations buyers. | Problem, user flow, human decision, controls, measured or modeled outcome and evidence status. | The claimed result cannot be sourced or clearly labeled. |
| **Workshop landing page** | Email 4 enterprise; email 3 mid-market. | Who should attend, workflow inputs, session outputs and what happens next. | It is being used as the first cold CTA. |

**Link rule:** Do not include links or attachments in the first cold email. Do not put links in a LinkedIn connection request or first message. Share a link later only when it directly supports the workflow being discussed.
