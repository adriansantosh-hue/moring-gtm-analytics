## 1. How We Actually Compete (honest read on the 3 named competitors)

Your three named competitors are **not one "kind" of competitor** — they sit in three different layers. This matters because each requires a different counter-position and targets a different buyer.

| | **Replit** | **Lyzr** | **Fiddler.ai** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Layer** | Agentic coding / app-building workspace | Enterprise agent-building platform (framework + governance) | AI observability, monitoring & GRC |
| **Core claim** | "Employees build apps/agents fast" (700 employees, 135 apps in 24h at Rokt) | "Build, orchestrate, govern enterprise agents end-to-end, on-prem/cloud, own your IP" | "Govern, observe & audit agents + models; SR 11-7, HIPAA, NAIC, EU AI Act audit trails" |
| **Buyer** | Business users + builders (bottom-up, low governance bar) | Platform/engineering teams (CIO/CTO, IT) | Risk/compliance/model-risk (CISO, CRO, Head of AI Governance) |
| **Moring's edge** | Replit is a **coding playground, not a governed substrate** — no real audit lake, no model-risk evidence, weak production controls | Lyzr gives you a framework + basic SSO/RBAC/audit, but **no forward-deployed engineering, no outcome-priced delivery, no regulated-workflow agents** (claims/AML/payments), no AWS Marketplace fast-lane | Fiddler does **monitoring/observability**, but **doesn't run your agents, doesn't govern the SDLC, doesn't build your workflow agents, doesn't deliver outcomes** — it observes; Moring governs + builds + delivers |
| **Where you win** | The moment the buyer says "we need it governed/production/auditable" — Replit dies at security review | When the buyer needs **delivered outcomes in a regulated workflow + FDE + outcome pricing**, not another framework to build on | When the buyer needs **governance that ships working agents + cost + delivery**, not just a monitoring pane |

**Key takeaway:** None of these three competes on **Moring's full model = AICP control plane + AI-DLC + AI Ops + business agents + forward-deployed engineering + outcome pricing**. Each wins only one layer. Your sales conversation should **reframe the category** from "pick an agent tool/platform/monitor" to "pick a governed substrate + a delivered, measured outcome."

**Weaponize against each:**
- **vs Replit:** "Replit helps people build agents fast. Who governs them, audits them, and gets them past your CISO and model-risk review?" (Deloitte/BCG data: governance & prepared processes are the #1 weakness — only 5% say processes are highly ready.)
- **vs Lyzr:** "Lyzr is a framework you build on — that's a DIY project with hidden integration cost and retrofitted governance. We deliver the platform **and** the governed workflow agents, outcome-priced, with an engineer embedded in your stack."
- **vs Fiddler:** "Fiddler observes and audits AI — but you still have to build the agents, govern the SDLC, and ship the outcome. We give you the whole governed substrate plus delivered agents and measured results."

## 2. The Moring ICP (reinforced + sharpened for US)

### 2.1 Core ICP (unchanged, confirmed by research)
- **Segment:** US mid-market (500–5,000 employees) + strategic enterprise (5,000+).
- **Industry:** banking/FS/payments/lending; insurance; healthcare payors/revenue-cycle; mission-critical ops (cloud/cyber/telecom/aviation/infrastructure); AI-driven engineering orgs; regulated shared-services.
- **Must-have conditions:** regulated or governance-sensitive workflows + POC→production stall + a "can pay" budget + an accountable workflow owner + a control buyer we can win.

### 2.2 The US market timing signal (why NOW)
**Deloitte (Aug 2026, 501 US leaders):**
- Only **15%** of US orgs have reached scaled, orchestrated, multi-agent adoption. **42% still testing; 43% expanding**.
- Fewer than half of US leaders are "ready" for agentic AI in almost every dimension. **Only 16% say business processes are prepared; only 5% highly prepared.**
- The two weakest areas: **workforce and business processes** — exactly the "operating workflow + outcome" gap Moring's FDE model fills.
- **31% expect half their processes redesigned around agents in 2 yrs → 74% in 4 yrs.** A multi-year wave is coming.

**BCG (Jul 2026, regulated industries):** regulated businesses need a **common platform** (orchestration, model access, evals, guardrails, memory, security, monitoring) or AI becomes fragmented and hard to govern. **Guardrails/eval/standards must be embedded from the start, not added after agents are in production** — this is literally Moring's "governance on by default" pitch.

**Regulatory:** SR 11-7 **superseded by SR 26-2 (Apr 2026)**; genAI/agentic **not yet in scope** but regulators "plan to issue an RFI." **Window to get governed before the rule lands = urgency lever for US banks.**

## 3. Intent Signals (what to look for — and where)

### 3.1 Hiring signals (highest intent — LinkedIn / career pages)
- **"Head of AI Governance" / "AI Governance Lead"** — live Type C buyer (Guardian Life example). They are actively building governance → prime target.
- **"Chief AI Officer" / "Head of AI" / "Head of Agentic AI"** — Type A budget/outcome owner.
- **"Head of Model Risk" / "VP AI Risk"** — SR 26-2-driven banks/insurers.
- **"VP Platform Engineering / DevEx / Head of AIOps / SRE Transformation"** — AI-DLC / AI Ops champions.
- **Internal "Forward-Deployed Engineer / AI Solutions Engineer" hires** — they tried to build it themselves (possible DIY pain / possible customer).

### 3.2 Public-pain / content signals
- Earnings/10-K/PR admitting **POC-to-production stalls**, "few agents in production," "AI cost is a black box."
- Blog/thought-leadership on **governance, model risk for LLMs, guardrails, AI audit trails, AI cost observability**.
- **Case studies of DIY agent builds** or security reviews stalling AI.
- Adoption of **agentic coding tools (Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot, Replit)** with no audit trail → AI-DLC wedge.

### 3.3 Technographic / stack signals
- **AWS/Azure/GCP presence** + AWS Marketplace agreements (can-pay, fast procurement).
- **Observability stack** present (Dynatrace, New Relic, Datadog, ELK, SolarWinds, Oracle EM) → AI Ops overlay wedge.
- **Data platforms** (Databricks/Snowflake) → AI-ready data / cost attribution angle.

### 3.4 Funding / revenue / can-pay
- Mid-market >$50M revenue, or strategic enterprise with committed AI spend; regulated operators are cash-rich.
- **Cloud EDP / Marketplace commitments** = can-pay + faster procurement signal.

### 3.5 The talent-gap signal (our strongest wedge) — hiring AI talent is slow
The single biggest operational signal for Moring: **they can't hire the AI talent to build it, and hiring takes too long.** This is our "plug in, finish fast" entry point.

- **Long/continuous AI roles open** — same "Head of AI / AI Engineer / ML Platform Engineer / AI Solutions Engineer" role posted for months, or reposted repeatedly. Means they have the budget line but can't fill it → they're stuck not because of intent, but because of **time-to-talent**.
- **"Slowness" signals in job posts** — required years of experience + security clearance + regulated-industry knowledge + on-prem work = months-long search. Regulated firms can't easily hire scarce AI talent (Deloitte: only 5% of US orgs are "highly prepared" — talent + process are the weakest areas).
- **Recruiters/agencies involved** — signals they've given up on organic hiring and are paying premium for speed.
- **They hired 1 AI lead but no team to execute** — one "Head of AI Governance" but no engineers to build the agents. The leader needs a partner who ships.
- **DIY failure / POC churn** — they tried building, burned months/quarters, nothing reached production → the "building it ourselves" bet has already failed.

**Why this is Moring's best angle:** these accounts have **budget + intent** but are **blocked by time**. Moring doesn't ask them to hire or build — **Moring plugs in a forward-deployed engineer who finishes fast and hands over the owned outcome.** We convert a 6–12-month hiring + build cycle into **weeks on a governed substrate**. Outcome-based, so they're not paying for headcount they can't hire.

## 7. What Else Is Our Strong Suit? (the full "why us" beyond outcome-pricing)

Beyond outcome-pricing and plug-in-fast, Moring has several under-leveraged strengths to lead with:

1. **Speed to value — the core strong suit.** Turn **months/quarters into weeks.** A paid, creditable POC + FDE on site + governed patterns that ship before developers arrive = they see a working governed agent in days, not a 9-month procurement + build cycle. **This is the #1 message.**

2. **Outcome-pricing de-risks the buy.** Baseline first, verify second, bill third — **the fee only triggers if the outcome ships.** For a budget-conscious buyer, this removes the "am I wasting $200K?" fear that kills deals. Almost no one else will commit their fee to a result.

3. **"Plug in, finish fast, hand it over" — we remove the hiring problem.** They can't hire AI talent (or it takes 6–12 months). Moring **doesn't ask them to hire** — we bring the forward-deployed engineer, finish the governed build, and **transfer source/runbooks/evals at handoff**. They get the outcome now without the hiring fight. **Outcome-based = they're not paying for headcount they can't hire.**

4. **No lock-in + you own it.** Deployed in *their* cloud/VPC; source, runbooks, evals, decision logs handed over. The anti-enterprise-vendor pitch regulated buyers trust. They keep the IP and the audit trail.

5. **Governance on by default, not retrofitted.** Policy at the substrate (OPA/Cedar), MCP scoping, audit lake with trace-ID forensics, agent registry + kill switch. This is what gets the CISO/model-risk sign-off that others can't clear.

6. **We deliver actual business agents, not a framework to build on.** Competitors (Lyzr, Replit, CrewAI) give you tools; SIs bill hours. Moring **ships governed, measured agents** (claims, underwriting, AML, payments, support, AIOps, engineering) with an engineer embedded with your SMEs.

7. **One substrate that compounds.** AICP + AI-DLC + AI Ops + business agents on the **same control plane** — you start with one wedge, expand on the same platform; **each new agent is cheaper than the last.** Buy-level speed, build-level control.

8. **Regulated-industry depth + compliance mapping.** SR 26-2, NAIC, MRM, FFIEC, HIPAA, SOC 2, ISO 27001 patterns baked in — we speak the model-risk/audit language that the buyer committee cares about, and we map our audit lake to their examiners' expectations.

9. **AWS-native fast lane.** Bedrock-native + AWS Marketplace-listed + in-VPC deployment. For AWS accounts, **procurement rides existing agreements** (shorter cycle) and security review is one they've already done.

10. **Forrester/Gartner-validated positioning.** "Oversight must live outside the agent's execution loop" (Forrester) and ">40% of agentic projects canceled by 2027" (Gartner) — Moring is positioned exactly where the market is heading.

**The one-line strongest message to lead every pitch with:**
> "Most AI is stuck at the demo because hiring, building, and governing take forever. Moring plugs in, finishes fast on a governed substrate in your cloud, ties our fee to a measured outcome, and hands you the keys. Weeks, not years — no lock-in."
