# Moring Outbound Analytics and Messaging Change

**Date:** 2026-09-19  
**Status:** Proposed for testing

## Executive Summary

Moring's outbound targeting and email infrastructure are working, but the current messaging is not generating conversations.

Prospects are receiving and opening the outreach, sometimes repeatedly, yet there are effectively no human email replies or qualified opportunities.

The proposed change is to retain the current targeting while replacing technical, platform-led language with simple, pain-led messaging and a low-risk pilot offer.

## 2. Diagnosis

The analytics indicate that:

- The accounts are relevant enough to earn attention.
- The emails are reaching inboxes.
- Prospects are opening the messages.
- The failure occurs after the open.

This means the immediate problem is not targeting or deliverability. It is the message's ability to turn attention into a reply.

---

## 3. Why the Previous Copy Failed

The previous messaging relied heavily on terminology such as:

- AICP
- Control plane
- Governed AI delivery
- Production-ready AI
- Identity and permissions
- Audit evidence
- Governance architecture

These terms may be accurate internally, but prospects do not necessarily understand them or immediately connect them to an urgent business problem.

The previous copy also:

- Explained Moring's technology before the buyer's problem.
- Repeated the same signal and platform description across multiple touches.
- Used long, abstract sentences.
- Asked recipients to understand a new product category.
- Lacked customer logos or measured case studies to support its claims.
- Did not make the next step feel simple enough.

The result was interest without action: prospects opened the messages but did not reply.

---

## 4. Proposed Messaging Change

Keep the existing:

- Account targeting
- Buyer selection
- Company signals
- Sending accounts
- Email infrastructure

Change the messaging from platform-led to pain-led.

### Previous approach

> Moring provides a governed AI control plane with identity, permissions, policy enforcement, and audit evidence.

### Proposed approach

> When clients run AI agents, they need to know what the AI accessed, what it did, and who approved it.

The new copy uses language that anyone can understand:

- Logs
- Tickets
- Spreadsheets
- Actions
- Approvals
- Reports
- Human decisions

It then offers a simple, low-risk next step:

- One workflow
- A 15-minute conversation to see whether it would be useful
- Free pilot
- Payment only if agreed value is delivered

## 5. Proposed LinkedIn Copy

### DM1 — Two Days After Connection

Hi {FIRST_NAME}, thanks for connecting.

As {COMPANY} scales its AI work on AWS, clients will ask: What did the AI access? What did it do? Who approved it?

Most teams still piece this together from logs, tickets, and spreadsheets. It becomes harder with every new AI agent.

We record every action, exception, and approval in one place. Your client's team still makes every final decision.

Open to a 15-minute conversation to see whether this would be useful for your client work?

### DM2 — Three Days Later

Once AI agents go live, clients ask: What did the AI do, and who approved it?

Most teams need engineers to search through logs and ticket systems for the answer. We turn that activity into a clear report that can be reviewed in minutes.

Your client remains in control of every decision.

Open to a 15-minute conversation to see whether this could make client reviews easier for your team?

### DM3 — Four Days Later

{FIRST_NAME}, if there is a fit, we can build a free pilot on one workflow. You only pay if it delivers the value we agree on.

Open to a 15-minute conversation to see whether a pilot like this would be useful for your team?

---

## 6. Why the Proposed Copy Is Better

### It starts with a recognizable problem

The prospect immediately understands the issue:

> What did the AI do, and who approved it?

### It avoids unfamiliar terminology

The recipient does not need to understand AICP, control planes, or AI-governance architecture.

### It uses operational language

Logs, tickets, spreadsheets, approvals, and reports are familiar to business and technical buyers.

### It replaces logos with insight

Moring does not yet have a strong customer-logo portfolio. The message therefore demonstrates credibility by accurately describing the buyer's operational problem.

### It preserves human control

The copy explicitly states that the client's team makes every final decision.

### It makes the next step specific

The request is not a broad transformation or platform purchase. It is a 15-minute conversation to determine whether the approach would be useful, followed by one workflow only if there is a fit.

### It reduces perceived risk

The pilot is free, and payment is tied to delivering agreed value.

### It creates an easy response

The recipient can answer a clear yes-or-no question without first interpreting Moring's product category.

## 7. Recommended Test

Run the proposed copy against the next 50–100 qualified prospects.

Keep the following unchanged:

- Target accounts
- Buyer personas
- Sending domains
- Sending volume
- Campaign timing

This isolates messaging as the test variable.

### Primary Metrics

1. Human reply rate
2. Positive reply rate
3. Qualified conversations
4. Pilots scoped
5. Meetings created
6. Opportunities created

Open rate should remain a secondary indicator. Replies and qualified conversations determine whether the new message works.

---

## Conclusion

Moring is successfully reaching the target audience, but the previous copy is not converting attention into conversations.

The proposed change does not abandon the current targeting strategy. It simplifies how Moring communicates its value.

The new message focuses on:

- A problem prospects already recognize
- A result they can understand
- Human control
- One workflow
- A low-risk pilot

The objective is to convert existing attention into measurable replies, conversations, and opportunities.
