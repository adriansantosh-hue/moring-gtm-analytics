# NAIC AI bulletin: an exam-readiness checklist for claims and underwriting AI

Free to use, copy and share. No signup. Draft v0.1 by Moring AI (moring.ai). Not legal advice. State rules differ: check your department's bulletin.

## What the bulletin says about exams

The NAIC Model Bulletin on the Use of Artificial Intelligence Systems by Insurers (adopted December 4, 2023) tells insurers that decisions made or supported by AI must follow existing insurance law, including unfair trade practices and unfair discrimination rules. Section 4 says that, in an investigation or market conduct action, an insurer can expect to be asked about:

- how it develops, deploys and uses AI systems, or any specific model or application
- the outcomes of those systems, including adverse consumer outcomes
- its governance framework, risk management and internal controls

This holds "regardless of the existence or scope of a written AIS Program."

Adoption: the NAIC's implementation map (status as of August 31, 2026) lists about half of U.S. jurisdictions as having adopted the bulletin (26 entries, including D.C.), with separate action tracked in other states such as California, Colorado, New York and Texas. Check the map for your states.

## Per-claim file checklist (claims triage example)

For each claim where an AI system prepared, ranked or routed work, the file should let a reviewer find:

- [ ] Which AI system and version touched it
- [ ] What data it used (and from which systems)
- [ ] What it produced (documents assembled, flags raised, priority set)
- [ ] The decision made, and the named person who owned it
- [ ] What evidence supported the decision, kept with the claim
- [ ] Whether the outcome was checked for adverse consumer impact, and how
- [ ] Whether a third-party vendor model was involved

## Program-level checklist

- [ ] Inventory: a current list of AI systems used in underwriting, claims, pricing and fraud, with owners
- [ ] Testing: how each system is tested before changes go live, including for unfair discrimination
- [ ] Governance: who approves a new AI use, who reviews it, how often
- [ ] Documentation: where a regulator would look, and who can produce it fast
- [ ] Third parties: what you require of vendors that supply AI, and how you check

Reminder: a faster cycle time helps nobody if the file cannot survive review. Governed means reviewable.

Sources: NAIC Model Bulletin: Use of Artificial Intelligence Systems by Insurers (content.naic.org); NAIC legal adoption map for the bulletin (status as of August 31, 2026).
