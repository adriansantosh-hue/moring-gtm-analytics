# SR 26-2 and agentic AI: what it says, what it does not, and what to do meanwhile

Free to use, copy and share. No signup. Draft v0.1 by Moring AI (moring.ai). Not legal advice. Check the primary sources below before you act.

## What was issued

- On April 17, 2026 the OCC, the Federal Reserve and the FDIC issued revised model risk management guidance (OCC Bulletin 2026-13, Federal Reserve SR 26-2, FDIC press release).
- It supersedes and replaces SR 11-7 (issued April 4, 2011).
- It takes a risk-based approach: model risk management should fit the size, complexity and model risk profile of the bank.
- It is described as most relevant to banking organizations with over $30 billion in total assets, and may still be relevant to smaller ones with significant exposure to model risk.
- The FDIC press release says the guidance "does not set forth enforceable standards or prescriptive requirements" and that non-compliance will not result in supervisory criticism.

## What it says about generative and agentic AI

- The guidance says generative AI and agentic AI models are novel and rapidly evolving, and so are not within its scope.
- Its principles apply to traditional statistical and quantitative models and to non-generative, non-agentic AI models.
- In a May 1, 2026 speech, Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Bowman said the revised guidance does not apply to generative or agentic AI, and that "going forward, we expect other risk-management and governance practices to support adoption of generative and agentic AI." She also said the Fed is working to update third-party risk-management guidance.

## What that means (Moring's reading, not the regulators')

1. Out of scope is not out of mind. Your bank's own risk management and governance practices still decide what is appropriate for tools the guidance does not cover. The guidance itself says so.
2. A window, not an exemption. There is no agent-specific model validation standard in this document. That leaves your governance practice to carry the weight.
3. Vendor and third-party AI is a live topic. Expect your third-party risk process to be asked about AI features in products you already buy.

## A 10-minute self-check for a bank using agents

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Do we have a list of every agent and the workflow it touches? | You cannot govern what you cannot list |
| Does each agent have one accountable business owner? | Accountability |
| Is there a named human decision on writes, money and customer data? | Human in control |
| Can we show, for one decision, what the agent accessed and who approved it? | Evidence |
| Is vendor-supplied AI in our third-party risk inventory? | Third-party risk is being updated |
| Do changes to prompts, models and tools go through testing and approval? | Change control |
| Can we switch an agent off in minutes? | Containment |

Sources: OCC Bulletin 2026-13 (April 17, 2026); Federal Reserve SR 26-2 and attachment; FDIC press release "Agencies Issue Revised Model Risk Guidance" (April 17, 2026); Federal Reserve speech by Vice Chair for Supervision Bowman on AI in the financial system (May 1, 2026).
